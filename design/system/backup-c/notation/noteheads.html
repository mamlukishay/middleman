Black is a note you have not played yet and is yours to play. The hand the app is playing goes #6b7280 — clearly quieter, still readable from the music stand. Green and red are the two answers; the red dot under a missed note is the mark the sheet keeps. Violet with a 3 px stroke is a note the song is waiting on. The two hand colours are only used where the sheet is showing you which hand is which.

The practice view has its own two: blue for the note the backing is on, magenta for the note you are holding — the same magenta as the key strip and the bar's LED. The bar highlight is violet again, at .30, because it is a place and not a note.
The roll · on dark
On the dark roll the hands swap roles with the sheet: amber is the right hand, blue the left, and the app's own hand drops to .45 rather than changing colour. A hit turns green and a miss becomes a dark red box with a red edge — the note is still there to read, it is only no longer yours. A cross is a note that was not in the music at all.

Rules: green means right and red means wrong, everywhere, and violet is never either — it is where you are. The white sheet takes a stronger green (#16a34a) than the dark chrome does, because --hit washes out on white; the miss red carries on both and is left alone. Nothing on the sheet is filled with the accent: amber is a playhead on the strip and a hand on the roll, never a note on paper.
