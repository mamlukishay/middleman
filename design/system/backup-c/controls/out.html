Out strip

Where the sound goes: the piano over MIDI, or the computer's own speakers. It is one block with a cut across it, not two buttons — the container owns the corners and the single continuous side, the halves own only their faces and the one pixel of seam between them. It stays one face high while the toggles beside it are the transport's square, and its name sits in the air above it rather than in the row, so the block itself is a clean row of faces.

The three states it is ever in
Out

Where the name sits
Out
the name — --cap 10, uppercase, .08em, dim the block — --btn-h 29, one face, both halves
Three lines: the top of the name, the top of the face, and the base line the block stands on. Between the name and the face is --capgap 3 plus the --h 5 and the 1 px of base the block puts under itself — the same distance, mirrored above, so the name can never crowd the face. It is all out of flow; the bar's top padding, 8 + --cap + --capgap + --h, is what reserves the line.
Not chosen. The other way round was to put the label in the flow, left of the block in dim 12 px, the way speed and vol read. It uses one convention for the whole bar, but it adds about 30 px to the row and puts a word between two blocks. The name above won: it costs the bar a line of padding it already reserves, and nothing in the row of faces has to move aside for a caption.

Rules: the strip is one block. Shared 2 px corners, one continuous 5 px side under both halves, a --seam hairline where they meet, and only one half ever down — the lit half drops the whole 5 px and covers the side in its own column, so the seam reads as a step rather than a gap. Both halves carry the same weight in both states (600), so picking a face never changes the strip's width and nothing beside it moves. The name is position:absolute, so it can never push the block down; the bar's top padding is what reserves the line it sits in.
