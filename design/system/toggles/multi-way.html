Anchors, not buttons, and it shows nowhere. The nav is three <a href>s, so it navigates before a single module has loaded — which costs .seg>a a duplicate of the whole .seg>button rule plus background, color, text-decoration:none and a flex box to centre the text an anchor would otherwise leave at the baseline. It is a two-way toggle wearing the wrong clothes. Tutor and Free practice are mutually exclusive, exactly one is lit, and the pair sits full width at the top of the sidebar — every property of the strip. What it is made of is two separate buttons with a 5px gap, so at a glance it reads as a strip with a seam that is five times too wide, and its on-state has to be written a fourth time (.modes2 button.on) because .tgl.on and .seg>button.on do not reach it. Both specimens are shown at the sidebar's real 180px, which is where the cost lands: “Free practice” does not fit on one line in half of it, so the pair stands 44px tall while every other control in the app is 30px. The Proposal drops the gap and the duplicate rule and reuses .seg: same markup count, one fewer idea.

Rules. A multi-way toggle is one strip with one border, one 6px radius on the outside and 1px --line seams between the halves — the two-way Out control with more halves, and it takes the same lit state: amber ground, #14161a ink, weight 600. Exactly one half is lit, always; there is no empty strip and no strip with two. It comes in two sizes and only two: 30px for a choice you act on (the nav), 24px for a choice about how to look (the view picker), with the smaller one on the darker #22262f ground. Halves may be links or buttons, and the two are required to be indistinguishable. Anything that behaves like this — exclusive, full width, one lit — should be built as a strip and not as buttons placed next to each other.
