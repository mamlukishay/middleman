Two-way toggle

The Out control: where the notes go, piano or computer. One strip, one border, two halves and a label plate built into the left end — the same element on all three laptop pages, mounted by one function. It is the app's only inline label, and the only place a <b> is a piece of chrome.

How the strip is built
Out:
One border, one radius, and the halves have neither. .seg carries the 1px --line border, the 6px radius and overflow:hidden; every child sets border:0;border-radius:0 and gets its shape by being clipped. So the rounding happens once, on the outside, and the seam between the halves is a single hairline from .seg>button+button — never two borders meeting. The label is a plate, not a caption: .seg>b is 11px/500 --dim on #20242c, a shade darker than the buttons, with align-items:stretch making it full height. The strip measures the same 30px as every button: a 16px line box, 6px of padding above and below, and the strip's own 2px of border.

Rules. A two-way toggle is one strip: one border, one 6px radius on the outside only, and halves that are separated by a single 1px --line and nothing else — no gap, no second border, no shadow. The half that is on is amber with #14161a ink at weight 600, exactly as a lit button is; more than one half may be lit when the state is genuinely both. The label lives inside the strip as a plate, 11px/500 dim on #20242c, and this is the only inline label in the app — everywhere else a label sits outside its control. Disabled is the one place the language breaks its own rule and swaps colour instead of only fading: --dim on #23272f with cursor:not-allowed, because a half that cannot be chosen has to be distinguishable from a half that simply is not chosen.
