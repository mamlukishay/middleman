Colour

Eight tokens in style.css carry the whole app; looper.css and learn.css add names of their own, several of which are the same hex under a second name. Six greys are never tokens at all — they are written as literals wherever a surface steps up off the panel. The one ink is #14161a, and it is a literal too.

Nothing says which name to reach for. Amber is --accent when a control is on and --rh when a note belongs to the right hand — the same pigment doing two jobs. --lh is worse than a synonym: looper.css declares it as a lane height (clamp(70px,…)) and learn.css re-declares it as this blue, and learn loads last.

Rules. Colour comes from style.css's eight tokens; a page-specific sheet may add a name but must not add a hex that is not already one of them — --rh, --lh, --hit, --miss, --rec, --ok and --dub are all re-namings of --accent, --play and two reds and greens. The six surface greys (#2a2f3a face, #333947 hover, #22262f inset, #20242c plate, #23272f dead, #3a3f4a off) are literals and stay literals; a new surface picks one of the six rather than inventing a seventh step. An on-state is --accent ground with #14161a ink at weight 600, and the ink is written out — only a red or blue ground (#recBtn.on, .bar.cur) takes #fff instead. On the white sheet two colours change and only two: the right hand goes to #b07d1a and a hit goes to #16a34a, because those two are the ones the dark palette loses on paper.
