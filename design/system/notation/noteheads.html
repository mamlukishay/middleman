Noteheads and playheads

Five views draw the same music: two engraved on white (practice sheet, staff and scroll) and three on the dark ground (roll, falling, lane rolls). One set of meanings runs through all of them — what the app plays, what you play, which hand, hit, missed — and two of the colours change value when the ground turns white.

Scroll view — the strip under a fixed line
.sline / .splayedthe line is 2px --accent at 85%; behind it the played side takes a #7c6fe0 wash fading from 0 to 13%
The scroll view is the only place the playhead is amber rather than #7c6fe0 — it is read from across the room, and on a strip that never stops moving the accent is the one colour that is unambiguously "now". The noteheads keep the staff view's colours.

Rules. Two colours carry the whole app: --play blue is what the app is playing and --you magenta is what you are playing, and where both are true at once magenta wins — you would rather see yourself than the machine. Under those, the hands take a colour each: right hand amber, left hand blue, the same two hexes as --accent and --play. A hand the app is covering for you is the same colour, quieter — 45% on the dark ground, #6b7280 on the sheet — never a different colour. Scoring overrides hand: hit is green, missed is red, and a missed note is hollowed rather than filled so it still reads as a note. On a white sheet two of those take their darker twins — amber becomes #b07d1a and green becomes #16a34a — because the dark-chrome values wash out on paper; blue, magenta and red carry on both grounds and are never changed. Playheads are #7c6fe0 everywhere except the scroll strip, which uses --accent, and the lane rolls, which use plain #aab2bf until a lane is recording and it turns --rec.
